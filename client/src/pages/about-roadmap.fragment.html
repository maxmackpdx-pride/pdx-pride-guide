AboutZ TM

Queer Portland, connected

Find a night out, a room, a gig, or people to make plans with. Zaylist puts useful parts of queer Portland in one place so you can spend less time searching and more time together.

Every city used to have one ugly, free public square for a room, a gig, something to give away or sell, a community looking for its people, or the person you locked eyes with and never saw again. Then that square moved onto Facebook and Instagram, where accounts get shut down, pages disappear, and queer nights are labeled adult content. That is what happens when the room belongs to somebody else.

Zaylist brings it back to our ground as one community-built home for Portland’s queer life. Find your plans on Eventz, explore local places and posts on Mapz, head out with Outzide, and find your people in Z/ List. My Hoüz, Giftz, Gigz, Sellz, and Mizzed each make room for a different part of everyday life.

Pride never ends. Find queer nights, everyday help, and people who know the city. Show up for each other.

Pride Guides 1.0 and 2.0

When the newspaper’s Pride guide left out many gay events, a new guide gathered 40 queer events so they would be included and easy to find. Community help doubled the second-year guide to 80, but it stayed seasonal and every listing still had to be managed by hand.

  • A public guide centered on queer Portland
  • Coverage for events the newspaper guide left out
  • 40 events published in the first Squarespace guide
  • 80 events published in the second-year guide
  • Community help expanding coverage
  • The founding proof that Portland’s queer community needed its own source, and that the guide should outlive Pride
ShippedSystem

Built by hand on Squarespace

Both early guides were Squarespace sites. Every listing, venue detail, flyer, correction, and update was handled manually. That workflow proved a year-round platform could not run the same way.

  • Manual event and venue research
  • Flyer, date, time, and link collection
  • A correction and publishing workflow
  • Manual entry could not support a year-round platform
  • Businesses already had websites they needed to maintain
  • Zaylist should absorb the work, not create more of it

The 2026 guide carried 160 Pride events, four times the first guide, and became the low-key beta for a year-round platform. EVENTZ is now one trusted place for queer Portland instead of chasing events across eight platforms, private groups, and invite-only apps.

Off Squarespace. Publish once.

How it operates

Trust & boundaries

Community utility, not a creator platform. Zaylist organizes useful information and member listings instead of manufacturing content for engagement.
Paid products stay in their lane. Clearly labeled commercial placement may appear in SELLZ. My Hoüz can charge property managers for professional housing tools and services. Events, places, community posts, and organic search are not pay-to-rank.
Access before extraction. Future revenue should add value without turning basic discovery into a content paywall.
An account protects participation. Names and accountability help keep spam out when someone posts or manages information.
Personal data is not inventory. Monetization should come from useful products and services, not selling member information.
ZayDark is opt-in, not shame-driven. The community will not be sanitized by pretending consenting adults do not do what consenting adults do. Adult-oriented visibility lives behind a clear toggle that each person can turn on or off.
FAQ

Good

Why does this feel like a classifieds board?+
Because useful queer life is bigger than an event feed. Zaylist brings together events, housing, gigs, free stuff, missed connections, outdoor plans, places, and community posts so people do not have to hunt across eight platforms.
How is this different from other event apps?+
Businesses can keep posting events on their own websites instead of managing another platform. Zaylist gathers those events into one place people can trust, and promoters can claim an event when they need to update it.
How do I list my event?+
Put it on your normal public event page first. Zaylist may find it automatically, or an account holder can submit it directly. Promoters can also claim an existing event and verified promoters can keep their own listings current.
Where can businesses pay?+
Commercial placement may be sold inside SELLZ, and property managers may pay for professional tools in My Hoüz. Payment does not move a business, event, place, or community post higher in the ordinary results.
Do I need an account to use Zaylist?+
Not to browse. An account is needed when someone wants to post, claim, manage, message, or otherwise participate. That keeps names attached to actions and makes spam harder.
Is Zaylist trying to replace Facebook groups?+
It is trying to make them less necessary. Housing, outdoor recreation, gifting, events, and local exchange should not disappear because someone missed an invite, left Facebook, or could not find the right group.
What is ZayDark?+
ZayDark is an optional view for adult-oriented parts of queer life. It stays behind a clear on-and-off control, so adults can choose what they see without asking the whole community to pretend it does not exist.
Is this staying in Portland?+
Portland comes first. The goal is to make Zaylist genuinely useful and trusted here before taking the model to other cities. Expansion comes after the local product works.
Who decides what comes down?+
Zaylist does, using the community rules, the context of what happened, and the safety of the people involved. Removal should come with a reason, and there should be a person to contact when the decision needs another look.
