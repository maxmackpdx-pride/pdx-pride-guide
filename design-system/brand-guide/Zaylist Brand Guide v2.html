01

The Mark

Primary asset

Anatomy

The wordmark is a single-weight open contour, not a filled letterform with an outline. The stroke is the logo. Any treatment that fills the counters destroys the neon-tube read the identity is built on.

Open contour

Uniform stroke, no taper. Counters stay unfilled at every size.

Sheared terminals

Every terminal cuts on a diagonal. No horizontal or vertical end caps.

The swoop

Underline arc carries the same spectrum and is part of the mark. Never detach it.

Trademark

™ sits at cap height off the final T, at 14% of cap height. Never scaled independently.

Primary lockup · full-color on black · the default expression in every medium that supports it.

City lockup

An addition to the primary lockup, not a replacement. As Zaylist scales past Portland, the market name sets beneath the swoop in the same open contour, and the spectrum runs continuously left to right across both lines.

City lockup · ZAYLIST PORTLAND · used where the market needs naming.

City cap height

0.35X, measured from the master art. Never larger, never smaller.

Position

Centered on the wordmark, 0.1X below the lowest point of the swoop. The swoop separates the two lines and is never removed.

Tracking

Letterspaced so the city spans about 80% of the wordmark width. Set to fit, never scaled to fit.

Rules

One city per lockup. Never stack two cities, never swap the city for a tagline, never use the city line alone.

Every other rule in this guide still applies: clear space is measured from the city baseline, minimum size is set by the city line's legibility, and the ™ stays on the wordmark.

02

Clear Space & Minimum Size

Measured in X

Define X, then never crowd it

X = the cap height of the Z. It is the only unit in this document. Clear space on all four sides is 0.5X minimum, and 1.0X preferred. The swoop's descender counts as part of the mark, measure from its lowest point, not from the baseline of the letters.

0.5X0.5X 0.5X0.5X

Digital minimum

120 px
wordmark width. Below this the counters close up on standard displays.

Print minimum

32 mm
wordmark width at 300 dpi. Screen-print and embroidery: 45 mm.

Below minimum

Use the Z
Swap to the Z rather than shrinking the wordmark past legibility.

03

Glow Specification

New in v2.0

The glow is part of the logo

A neon mark reproduced without its bloom reads as a thin outline and loses every bit of its energy. This section did not exist in v1.0 and is the most common source of off-brand output.

Digital, CSS / SVG

Core stroke100% opacity, 1.0X width
Inner bloomblur 0.35X · 70% opacity · stop color
Outer bloomblur 1.4X · 34% opacity · stop color
Atmosphereblur 4X · 12% opacity · stop color
Blend modescreen (never multiply)

X here = stroke weight, not cap height. Three bloom passes minimum; two reads flat.

Print & physical

Halo is baked into the supplied raster art, do not add effects on top of it.
On garments, print the core stroke only. Glow does not survive screen-print.
Never recreate the glow with a drop shadow or an outer stroke.
Never apply the glow in a color other than the local stop color.

✓ Correct, bloom present, core legible.

✕ Wrong, glow stripped, mark goes inert. Only acceptable as the deliberate mono variant in §04.

04

Monochrome

Constrained reproduction

When color isn't available

Use monochrome for single-color print, embroidery, laser etch, fax-grade documents, and any partner placement that mandates one ink. Monochrome is a fallback, never a stylistic preference, if full color is reproducible, use full color.

Signal White

#FFFFFF on any ground darker than #333. The primary mono variant.

Void Black

#000000 on any ground lighter than #CCC. Documents, invoices, contracts.

Single-hue tints (an all-cyan or all-magenta wordmark) are not an approved variant. It is full spectrum, white, or black.

05

Backgrounds

Contrast floor

Dark is the home field

The spectrum is calibrated for emissive display against near-black. The full-color mark requires a ground at or below 18% luminance. Anything brighter and the bloom washes out.

✓ #000, ideal

✓ #14141A, approved

✕ mid grey, bloom lost

✓ light ground → mono black

Approved

Solid grounds at or below 18% luminance.
Photography that is genuinely dark and low-detail behind the mark.
Light grounds, switching to the mono black variant.

Not approved

Any ground carrying its own gradient or spectrum.
Mid-tone flats between 18% and 80% luminance in any variant.
Busy photography, patterns, or texture behind the full-color mark.

Misuse

Shown, not listed

Also prohibited

Reordering or reversing the spectrum stops.

10

Photography & Imagery

Real rooms, real people

Shot at night, lit by the room

Zaylist photography looks like someone who was actually there took it. Available light, high contrast, deep blacks, colored practicals. No studio backdrops, no stock smiles, no staged diversity.

Treatment

Crush the blacks so the image sits into the near-black page.
Let colored practicals carry the hue. Do not correct neon to neutral.
Grain is welcome. Noise reduction that smooths skin is not.
Poster art sits in a well with a 4 px accent floor and a faint scanline.

Limits

No stock photography of generic crowds.
No illustrated or AI-rendered people standing in for community.
No spectrum gradient laid over a photograph.
No identifiable face in a safety or consent context without consent on file.

Aspect

3:2 event hero · 1:1 poster well · 16:9 venue banner.

Overlay

Bottom scrim to rgba(0,0,0,.85) before any type lands on an image.

Fallback

No image? Use the accented glass card with a condensed title. Never a placeholder graphic.

11

Voice & Tone

How it sounds

Say the thing. Then stop.

Zaylist writes like a guy who already knows the city and is telling you where to go. Straightforward, slight bro lean, a little dry. Short sentences, concrete details, no hype. We give people what they need to decide whether to go, and we never editorialise about who they are.

Write this

"Doors at 9. Cash for the door, cards at the bar. Back room opens after the first set."

"Sober space. No alcohol served, BYO not allowed."

"Wheelchair accessible entrance on the alley side."

Not this

"Get ready for an unforgettable night of pure magic!"

"A safe space for everyone to be their authentic selves."

"Join our vibrant community of amazing humans."

Lead with the practical fact: time, door, price, access.
Use the community's own words for itself. Never soften them.
Sentence case for body, uppercase only in display and chrome.
Ranges read "Jul 16 to 19", never with a dash glyph.
No em dashes anywhere in product or marketing copy.
No exclamation marks in UI text.
No corporate euphemism: "authentic", "vibrant", "journey", "curated".
No speaking for a group we are describing.

Scene vernacular

Dry does not mean flat. "Yas" and "gurl" are on-brand and land well in stickers, empty states, toasts and board taglines. One per screen at most, and never inside functional UI: no buttons, labels, nav items or form errors. If it would be embarrassing on an error message, it does not go there.

The name is the joke

Said out loud, Zaylist sounds like the list, the fancy one you want to be on. The pun lives in the phrasing, not the spelling: build the sentence so the reader hears it. This is the closest thing the brand has to a tagline mechanic, so it earns a place in heroes, promoter pitches, submission confirmations and share cards.

Write this

"You're on Zaylist."

"Is your party on Zaylist?"

"Get on Zaylist."

Not this

Respelling it to force the joke: Zay'List, Zay List, ZayList.

Leaning on it without the setup: "Zaylist is the list."

Three of them on the same screen. It stops being a wink and becomes a bit.

Always spelled Zaylist. One word, one capital, no apostrophe.
Set it up with "on" or "your". That is what makes the ear hear "the list".
Never respell the name to make the joke land.
Never in nav, buttons, form copy, legal text or the app store listing.

Accessibility

Non-negotiable

Neon on black is a contrast problem we already solved

The palette is bright on purpose, which makes the failure modes specific: dark neons as type, colour as the only signal, and motion that cannot be switched off. All three are handled by rule, not by judgement.

RequirementFloor
Body text on ink7:1
Large text and chrome4.5:1
Non-text UI and borders3:1
Focus ring2 px #00FFFF, 3 px offset
Touch target44 px
Body size16 px minimum

Rules

Violet #8800FF and blue #0044FF are surface colors only. As type they swap to #AA66FF and #4488FF.
Day color always ships with a text label. Color never carries the meaning alone.
Calm mode is reachable in two taps from anywhere in the product.
Never place body copy directly on a neon fill.
Never remove the focus ring. Restyle it if it clashes.
13

Trademark & Assets

Before you ship

The mark is owned. The community is not.

Trademark

ZAYLIST™ carries the ™ symbol at cap height, trailing the final T, at one quarter of the cap height. It appears on the first or most prominent use in any piece. It is never scaled independently, never set below 8 px, and never omitted from the primary lockup.

First or most prominent use carries the ™.
Never recreate the ™ in a different typeface.

Co-branding

Partner lockups sit side by side on a shared baseline with a 1 px hairline divider at 1X, both marks optically matched by cap height rather than bounding box. Zaylist leads on Zaylist properties and follows on partner properties.

Divider is #2B2B2B at 1 px, full mark height.
Never lock the Z tile against another logo without clear space.
