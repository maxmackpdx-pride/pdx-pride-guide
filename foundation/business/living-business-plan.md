## Executive view

### Operating posture

Zaylist is a one-founder moonshot operating under severe founder-resource constraints. The plan must therefore run on two tracks at once:

1. Protect the founder's essential personal stability and capacity.
2. Execute the smallest credible validation path that can produce verified adoption, revenue evidence, or a decisive learning quickly.

The business plan must not assume funding arrives, treat fundraising as rescue, or ask the founder to sacrifice housing, food, healthcare, essential transportation, taxes, or debt minimums for speculative growth.

### Current purpose

Zaylist is a founder-led Portland platform helping people discover events, places, housing, work, gifting, connections, and community without reducing queer life to a once-a-year guide or a nightlife-only product.

### Current strategic objective

Reach 1,000 verified Zaylist downloads within a 90-day period by proving meaningful adoption in Portland.

Status: Founder direction. The verified-download definition, authoritative analytics source, and official measurement window remain unresolved.

### Current business thesis

If Zaylist becomes a trusted recurring utility for Portland's queer communities, it can create value through local discovery, participation, verified community information, and aligned commercial relationships. Revenue models must preserve trust and cannot outrank usefulness, consent, accessibility, safety, or truthful representation.

## Problem

Queer community information is fragmented across social platforms, venue pages, private chats, outdated listings, and products that often suppress or poorly contextualize adult, kink, or sex-positive community life.

Evidence status: Founder experience and product thesis. External validation and user research remain incomplete.

## Solution

Zaylist combines practical local discovery and community utilities within one governed platform. The product should help people understand what exists, what is current, who it serves, how to participate, and what actions are available.

The 90-day adoption plan is not yet complete. It requires:

- A clear reason to install or save Zaylist rather than visit once.

## Revenue model

No revenue model is accepted merely because it is possible.

Candidate paths for validation:

- Aligned sponsorships.
- Clearly disclosed advertising that matches product trust and design standards.
- Paid promoter, organizer, venue, or business tools that do not reduce public usefulness.
- Grants, competitions, or non-dilutive programs.
- Other models proposed through evidence and founder review.

Unknowns: willingness to pay, pricing, unit economics, sponsor demand, operating cost per active user, and community acceptance.

## Competition and alternatives

The plan must compare Zaylist against the actual alternatives people use: social platforms, search, venue sites, event platforms, private messages, community groups, spreadsheets, word of mouth, and doing nothing. Competitor claims require current sources and must distinguish direct products from behavioral substitutes.

### Scenario discipline

- **Survival case:** No outside money arrives. Preserve essentials, pause noncritical costs, maintain only the product reliability and work needed for the fastest credible validation or revenue test.
- **Base case:** Small revenue or non-dilutive support arrives. Fund only measured acquisition, core reliability, and the highest-confidence conversion barrier.
- **Moonshot case:** Adoption materially accelerates or suitable capital becomes available. Scale only after measurement, ownership, compliance, and operational capacity are ready.

The moonshot case may motivate the work. It may not be used as the cash-flow assumption.
