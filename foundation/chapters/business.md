## Why an LLC may fit

An Oregon single-member LLC can create a state-law entity boundary, clarify ownership, support contracting and business banking, and make expenses and revenue easier to account for. Oregon describes LLC members as having limited liability for LLC debts, while the IRS generally treats a one-owner LLC as disregarded for federal income tax unless another tax classification is elected.

An LLC is not a magic shield. Personal guarantees, mixed finances, misconduct, inadequate records, taxes, and contracts signed personally can still create personal exposure. Forming an LLC also creates filing, renewal, privacy, bookkeeping, and compliance work.

## Formation decision gate

Form the LLC when one or more real triggers justify the recurring burden:

- Zaylist is accepting meaningful recurring revenue, sponsorship, or paid partnerships.
- A contract, platform, bank, insurer, grant, or partner requires an entity.
- Tucker needs a separate business bank account and bookkeeping boundary.
- The platform is taking on material contractual, operational, privacy, event, employment, or vendor risk.
- A funding conversation requires a clean ownership and records package.
- The cost and maintenance are affordable without endangering personal essentials.

Wait when the LLC would exist mainly for appearance, the name/ownership plan is unresolved, required public addresses create an unmanaged privacy risk, or filing and professional costs would compete with food, housing, healthcare, debt minimums, or essential product reliability.

## Oregon path

1. Confirm the intended legal name and search Oregon records.
2. Decide member-managed versus manager-managed structure.
3. Choose a compliant Oregon registered agent and privacy-safe public addresses.
4. File Articles of Organization with the Oregon Secretary of State.
5. Create a written operating agreement even for one member.
6. Obtain an EIN when needed for banking, employees, excise tax, or the chosen operating setup.
7. Open a dedicated business account and stop mixing new business transactions with personal spending.
8. Establish bookkeeping categories, receipt storage, reimbursement rules, and monthly close.
9. Confirm Oregon/local registrations, taxes, licenses, insurance, contracts, and privacy obligations relevant to actual operations.
10. Calendar the Oregon annual report anniversary and required renewals.

Oregon currently lists a $100 Articles of Organization processing fee and a $100 annual LLC report fee. Filing information is generally public, so privacy choices must happen before filing, not after.

## Capital ladder

Prefer the least complicated money that proves the most:

1. Reduce or defer nonessential costs.
2. Validate demand and verified downloads.
3. Earn customer, sponsor, listing, advertising, or partner revenue consistent with trust.
4. Pursue suitable grants, competitions, or non-dilutive programs.
5. Consider small, clearly repayable debt only against a credible repayment source.
6. Consider regulated crowdfunding or private investment only with a defined entity, ownership records, measurement, financial statements, risk disclosure, and securities counsel.

Selling equity, SAFEs, notes, revenue interests, or other investment rights can involve securities laws even when the investor is a friend or community member. Public fundraising language can also create legal consequences. No agent may solicit investors, promise returns, circulate offering terms, or call money an investment without Tucker's explicit authorization and qualified legal review.
