# Make PlaceZ Building Images

Produce a standalone building artwork matching the four Holocene, Stag, Alberta Rose, and Eagle images at the November 9, 2026 checkpoint captioned “More photographic buildings, with more physical wear in the paper and print.” Stop before card composition.

Use **3D wireframing** as the key term in instructions, generation prompts, and descriptions. Tucker confirmed this wording. It means tight, fine, blueprint-like spatial grids, triangulated meshes, planes, and perspective construction lines extending around the actual building edges and through the visible sky. Allocate about 70% of the added colored linework to the primary category accent and 30% to its complementary accent. Do not reduce this to generic glowing outlines, neon tubes, or a rectangular border.

## Reconstruction boundary

**Choose photos to reconstruct the actual building, not simply the first three search results.** Each selected view must establish a needed architectural detail or resolve an uncertainty in another view. Tucker clarified that combining angles into a face-forward storefront with road below and sky above requires some imagination: reasonable perspective correction, compositional reconstruction, inferred ground extent, and atmospheric sky treatment are allowed. Do not confuse that latitude with permission to invent concrete objects or factual detail.

**Never invent objects, signs, text, or anything in the windows.** Preserve the photographed building identity, facade/window layout, existing objects, sign wording/design/size/placement, and visible window contents. Do not add or redesign logos, fixtures, furniture, products, posters, people, vehicles, plants, or other scene objects. A venue name, listing text, style example, or prior generation is not proof that a sign or object exists in a particular place. Perspective-based estimates may make the composition coherent; they must not create new openings, signs, objects, or window contents. Distinguish inferred geometry/atmosphere from photographed facts and disclose material uncertainty.

Do not turn unreadable lettering into guessed words or pseudo-text. Seek a clearer photo when needed; otherwise preserve the source's lack of legibility. Keep hidden object/sign/window-content details unknown rather than filling them with plausible content. The prescribed wirework, color accents, and print/paper collage are visual treatment layers, never new physical signage or scene objects.

## Establish each place

1. **Determine the place type and intended card color first.** Verify the current category and its assigned token, the actual card-edge color if it differs, and the complementary accent. Use that intended card color for the primary wirework and all torn-paper edges. Do not choose a palette from personal taste or from another venue. Record the exact source/token values before generation.
2. Follow **Find and verify the source photos** below. Deliberately select multiple distinct authentic reference photographs for complementary architectural coverage, aiming for 3–5 useful views when available rather than stopping at the first three results: the full front, a second angle, and close details of the facade/windows. A crop of one photo is not another independent source. Record dates, creator/credit, URLs, and licenses, and reconcile conflicts or changes between dates. If available evidence does not establish the windows or front, obtain another view or ask for it rather than make them up.
3. Record visible facade landmarks: roofline and outer corners, each window group and pane division, visible contents/reflections/curtains, door positions, columns, signage, foreground objects, and the boundary between sidewalk and blacktop. Mark hidden or illegible details as unknown.
4. Use the genuine sources together as architecture authority. A prior generated image is style evidence only. Ask only when an unresolved identity, required view, or rights issue is critical to the requested result and cannot be resolved from available sources. Otherwise proceed with supported details and reasonable compositional reconstruction, label the remaining uncertainty, and never invent hidden objects, signs, text, window contents, or new architectural features.
5. Use an approximately 3:2 landscape composition unless the user asks otherwise. Make a **face-forward front elevation**, not an oblique corner view. Compose the building's facade, roofline, and outside edges face-forward, with road/ground below and substantial visible atmospheric sky above. Use the multiple source angles to establish the real setting; reasonable perspective/composition work may infer ground extent and sky around the storefront. Do not add objects, road markings, signs, or window contents as part of that reconstruction. Do not introduce roadway solely to accommodate the rainbow or replace a user-requested sidewalk treatment with a road. Do not replace the sky with a black mesh slab or crop it out. Overlay fine wireframes while allowing the sky to read behind them. Keep the facade lower in the frame if needed to provide sky without inventing more building. **No cars or other vehicles.** Remove them using the additional source views to resolve covered details; reasonable surface reconstruction does not permit invented openings, objects, signs, or window contents. Hold the approved crop and scale across later passes.
6. For the historical four, use the documented palette snapshot as context; verify current values for a new venue. Do not infer a permanent category mapping from a generated image.

## Find and verify the source photos

Do the sourcing rather than asking Tucker to gather photos that are available. Keep an exact, local copy of every real photograph used so it can accompany the first result.

1. **Resolve the exact place and listing.** Read the selected PlaceZ listing or current project record for the venue name, city, street address, branch, website, and category. Cross-check the official venue website and visible address/signage. For a renamed, relocated, closed, or multi-location business, separate each address and period. Never use a similarly named venue, a different branch, or the old address silently.
2. **Look at supplied photos first, then official sources.** Inspect relevant user uploads and on-file originals. Search the venue's official site, gallery, contact/location page, and official press/media kit. Official photos are useful for identity and current appearance, but being official does not itself grant reuse rights. Use exact-name queries with the city/address and terms such as `exterior`, `storefront`, `front entrance`, `windows`, or `interior`; search an established former name only when the requested period requires it.
3. **Find usable alternatives and corroboration.** Search Wikimedia Commons or other open-license archives, the photographer's original page, local historical collections, and authorized licensed photo sources. Open each original source page rather than trusting search thumbnails, snippets, or reposts. Official social posts, local reporting, listing photos, maps, and street-level imagery can help corroborate identity, facade geometry, or dates when accessible under their terms; they are not automatically reusable image assets. Do not scrape restricted images, remove watermarks, bypass access controls, or treat a screenshot as a workaround for reuse restrictions. Prefer a clearly licensed or user-authorized source when rights are uncertain.
4. **Build a complementary set, usually 3–5 distinct photographs, chosen for reconstruction rather than search order.** Identify the missing evidence before each additional search. For every selected photo, note which roofline/corner, facade section, window group, doorway, sign, or ground boundary it establishes and which uncertainty it resolves. Replace redundant or unhelpful results; a photo count alone never completes sourcing. Seek a complete front elevation including the roofline and outer corners, a second angle that exposes depth or occluded facade sections, and close views of the storefront, entrances, signage, window groups, panes, and what is actually visible through the windows. Use relevant interior photos only to corroborate identifiable window/door relationships; do not project a separate interior view into the exterior windows. When ground is visible, use a wider authentic view to identify its actual surfaces and boundaries. Use this evidence to reconstruct coherent road/ground geometry in the face-forward composition. Do not mistake compositional latitude for permission to add scene objects or to create a road solely for the rainbow. A crop, mirror, upscaled copy, or repost of one photograph is not a second independent view. Stop when the coherent set supports the requested reconstruction, not when a quota is reached. Do not force every angle if the available set already establishes the required details.
5. **Check identity and time across the set.** Match visible landmarks and addresses before combining views. Record capture dates separately from posting/upload dates, and mark unknown dates explicitly. Compare signage, windows, renovations, and adjacent buildings. Do not blend older and newer facade states as if they coexisted. Use the period requested by the user; if none is specified, prefer the most recent coherent, corroborated set. Do not label a building current based only on an undated image or a recent repost. Ask which period/location is intended only when a material conflict remains unresolved.
6. **Keep provenance and rights with each file.** Record the original page URL, direct image URL when available, creator/credit, capture date or date uncertainty, posting date if relevant, retrieval date, license/permission and its source, local filename, and the architectural detail it supports. Distinguish licensed generation/delivery inputs from view-only corroboration. Preserve attribution and ShareAlike requirements. A paid license, permission request, or contacting a photographer is a separate action requiring the applicable user authorization. A user upload is a supplied reference, not proof that it may be publicly redistributed. Do not invent a license or assume that a generated derivative erases rights obligations.
7. **Inspect the actual image files before generating.** Obtain the best legitimately available resolution, view every chosen image, and reject unrelated listings, unreadable thumbnails, AI-generated architecture, or images too cropped to establish the necessary details. Retain the originals unchanged. Use genuine sources as architecture authority and the bundled collage/endpoints only as treatment references. If source geometry cannot support a required front elevation, obtain another authorized view. Ask for a user-provided photo only after reasonable available sourcing is exhausted and the missing detail is critical.
8. **Show the sources with the first result.** Attach the real source photographs actually used, clearly labeled with venue/location, view, credit, date or uncertainty, and license/permission information. Separate them from generated artwork and style references. Links alone do not satisfy this first-delivery requirement. Choose inputs that can be delivered as authorized; if a restriction prevents showing an essential source, find a permitted alternative or surface that specific blocker before presenting the artwork as complete. Do not silently omit the sources or claim unsupported details are verified.

## Apply the treatment after Step 1 color selection and source collection

### Establish the photographic HDR building

Create a straight-on, eye-level architectural image grounded in the real reference. Keep verticals controlled, materials believable, tonal range rich, and texture crisp. Reconstruct the photographed proportions, window groups and pane divisions, doors, and existing signage faithfully; do not settle for a merely recognizable or generic cyberpunk facade. Preserve sign wording, design, size, and placement only where the photographs establish them. The initial concepts used subtle category-colored holographic edging; this is a starting state, not the final wireframe density.

Use the original photograph to guide perspective correction. Do not pretend that a newly synthesized front elevation reveals documentary facts absent from an angled source. Avoid painterly smoothing, exaggerated game-render surfaces, replacement window contents, or a heavy stylization of the entire facade.

### Verify against the original photographs

Compare the base directly with all selected real photographs at a matched crop and scale, particularly window counts, pane divisions, visible contents, and each sign's wording, design, size, and placement. Check each depicted object, architectural feature, sign, text detail, and window content against its supporting photograph. Remove or correct unsupported concrete additions before covering the base with decoration; visual plausibility is not evidence. Assess inferred perspective, ground extent, and sky separately as compositional reconstruction, not verified documentary detail. Preserve current evidence of occlusion rather than claiming hidden details are verified. For the original four, the source photographs date from 2015–2019; do not call them current building conditions.

Carry the later pre-Xerox styling requirements into the base before Step 3:

- Add **3D wireframing**: tight, fine, blueprint-like planes, grids, triangulated meshes, and perspective construction lines in the sky and around the building's outside edges. Trace the real roofline and outer silhouette, not just the image rectangle.
- Keep most line density off the facade and especially off important windows and signage. Dense spatial detail belongs in the surrounding negative space.
- Allocate roughly 70% of the colored wire treatment to the venue's assigned category color and 30% to its complementary accent. This applies to added colored wirework, not the whole image or natural building colors.
- Use bright token colors if appropriate, but no luminous neon tubes, thick ropes, or broad glowing perimeter bands. Retain genuine neon signage already present in the source; the restriction concerns newly added decoration.
- Apply the **continuous textured Zaylist-logo spectrum** over existing blacktop/asphalt, following the approved rainbow road correction (source-recipe reference: `references/approved-rainbow-road.md`). Match the approved Sports Bra result: red/pink → orange → yellow → green → cyan → blue → violet/purple, approximately 60% overlay-strength guidance with asphalt texture and dark shading retained. Do not prompt for reflected lights, faint isolated puddles, or the old repeated cyan/yellow/magenta/orange sequence. Do not add or enlarge road solely for this treatment, change another surface into asphalt, or spill onto sidewalks, buildings, windows, or sky. Omit when blacktop is absent unless the user explicitly authorized another existing surface for that image.

**Task-specific exception, not a recipe-wide change:** For the Pizza Thief/#6 image revision on November 11, 2026, Tucker explicitly allowed the rainbow on the existing sidewalk instead and rejected the added road. For this requested Pizza Thief revision, remove the unwanted added road, preserve the real sidewalk's extent/material/texture, and overlay the same approved logo/prior-image rainbow there. General latitude to reconstruct road/sky does not override this specific requested treatment. Do not carry sidewalk permission into other venues or future images without a new explicit instruction.

### Add imperfect black Xerox overprint

Treat the accurate color image as the print underneath. Add a physically plausible imperfect black photocopy pass on top: uneven toner, broken ink, scuffs, fine scratches, misregistration, grain, and localized print loss. Retain the building's photographic material detail, wire colors, and muted existing-ground color treatment. Avoid a uniform dirty filter, full monochrome conversion, or grunge that hides the windows.

### Build the physical collage from the two supplied references

Inspect `assets/step-4-user-collage-bridge.jpeg` and `assets/step-4-user-collage-map.jpeg` directly. Use their layered black paper, irregular ripped edges, exposed cream/tan fibers, creases, abrasions, worn ink, halftone scraps, translucent scuffed tape, and sparse metallic staples as the physical language. Let tears overlap naturally and cast restrained paper-depth shadows. Favor the perimeter and corners so the real building stays prominent.

Translate the reference materials, not their content. Do not copy their bridge, map pins, speaker, slogan, binder rings, or map layout into every venue image. Small print/registration accents may support the collage without becoming a new brand logo. Avoid smooth plastic panels, clean vector tears, a generic speckle border, or evenly repeated decoration.

**Make the white torn paper match the primary category accent.** Recolor the references' exposed white, off-white, cream, and tan paper, torn edges, and fibers to the verified primary category/card accent established in Step 1, not the 30% complementary wire color. Tucker reaffirmed this using the bundled map collage on November 11, 2026. Retain texture, thickness, fibers, and darker shaded variations; do not turn paper into smooth plastic or neon tubes. Do not recolor pale brick, white windows, signs, logos, tape, or metallic fasteners. The cream edges in the historical endpoint are a material reference only; Tucker's later explicit color instruction supersedes them.

### Increase photorealism and physical wear

Make the building read more like a real architectural photograph while making the paper and print feel more handled: frayed fibers, layered delamination, cracked toner, creased tape, worn corners, scratched staples, and contact shadows. Concentrate the added damage on the collage and print surface rather than inventing structural decay on the actual venue.

Compare with the four bundled Step 5 endpoints. Match the balance: large photographic facade, dense fine sky/edge wireframe, restrained ground color, and tactile black collage framing. Do not make “more wear” mean a less photographic building or an obscured facade. Preserve the same crop, palette, and source landmarks across corrections.
